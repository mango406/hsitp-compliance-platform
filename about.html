About the Repository

Portolan

A portolan was a navigator's chart — compiled not from theory but from the accumulated reports of pilots who had actually made the crossing. It showed coastlines, harbours, and hazards, and it was valued because it was practical.

This repository is built on the same principle: a working map of AI regulation, assembled from primary legal sources, for organisations that need to cross jurisdictional boundaries.

01

What it covers

Laws, regulations, and official guidance across four regulatory themes — data governance, cross-border data flows, algorithmic transparency, and licensing & certification — mapped consistently across jurisdictions.

Every entry includes a plain-language overview, effective dates, the key articles that matter in practice, penalty structures, and direct links to official texts and recognised translations.

02

Who it is for

Legal, compliance, and strategy teams at organisations of any size seeking to understand a regulatory environment before entering a new market or deploying an AI-powered product.

Built with founders and early-stage teams in mind — the people least likely to have in-house counsel and most likely to need a starting point rather than a billable hour.

03

How it is structured

The same four questions are asked of every jurisdiction. That consistency is what makes genuine comparison possible — without it, the result is simply a list of laws rather than a map.

Where a single regulation is relevant to more than one theme, it appears under each, with the articles most relevant to that context brought forward.

Sources and method

Entries are drawn from official government publications wherever available. Where a regulation exists only in its original language, the repository links both the official text and a recognised English translation — most often from Stanford's DigiChina project or China Law Translate for PRC instruments, and EUR-Lex for EU law.

Key articles are selected rather than exhaustive. A statute may contain seventy provisions; the ten that determine whether an organisation is compliant are the ones reproduced here. Where an article is omitted, the primary source link remains the authoritative reference.

Some areas move faster than others. United States AI regulation in particular has seen substantial change within single legislative sessions — Colorado repealed and replaced its AI Act in 2026 before the original had taken effect. Entries should be verified against primary sources before being relied upon.

Important notice

This repository provides general informational guidance only and does not constitute legal advice. It is not a substitute for qualified counsel in any jurisdiction. Regulations change frequently — always verify against the linked primary sources before making compliance decisions.
